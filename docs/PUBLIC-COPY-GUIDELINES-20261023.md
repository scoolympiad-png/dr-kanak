# Public-facing website copy rules — October 2026

These rules apply to visitor-facing page content, SEO descriptions, banners, cards, accessibility text and CTAs.

1. Speak to patients, parents, visitors and families — never to an auditor, developer or hospital administrator.
2. Never publish internal status terms such as *verified core, pending verification, launch department, source-backed, brochure-backed, approval required, content gate, staging, template transcription, reference framework,* or project names like *Patient OS* unless the visitor is genuinely buying or using that product.
3. Use short banners: one clear headline, one short sentence, and a primary action; optional secondary action. Avoid dense hero paragraphs, audit notes and stacks of claims.
4. Keep medical advice responsible. Give concise, supportive guidance; clearly distinguish education from individual diagnosis or vaccination prescriptions. Preserve urgent-care directions where appropriate.
5. Label only services actually offered. Avoid unverified opening hours, schedules, medical outcomes, stock availability, vaccination dose schedules and promises of live reminders.
6. Every doctor/patient/child photo must be authentic, properly licensed and approved for public website use. For identifiable children or patients, confirm appropriate guardian/patient consent before publishing.
7. Write readable text: body 16–18px, secondary at least 14px where layout permits. Accessibility and keyboard use matter.
8. Prefer the real Mamta / Dr Kanak images and official marks. No AI substitution for a doctor's identity.
9. International research and governmental guidance can inform copy, but external studies do not turn into personalized clinical advice. Avoid publishing internal sourcing procedures.
10. Keep internal evidence inventories, permissions and source dates inside /docs/ instead of visitor sections. Link to useful public-facing content, not internal checklists.
11. Check every page as a real visitor before release: what is offered, how to contact the hospital, how to book, what to read next.
12. Keep each update narrow, verifiable, deployable and reversible.

Internal note: private clinical integrations should remain explicitly inactive until properly configured, but do not turn this operational fact into a large page banner. Use a brief truthful visitor-facing status when relevant.
